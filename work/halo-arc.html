Most bone-conduction headsets are designed around a narrow, medicalised idea of fit, and it shows the moment someone tries to wear one over natural afro hair, glasses, or a head size outside the average. This page walks through how Halo Arc rebuilds that fit mechanism from research up, through sketching, prototyping and CAD, to a pull-to-slide headset validated by hand before it ever reached engineering.

01 · The Problem

Standard headband and bone-conduction designs clamp at fixed points that assume short, straight hair and an average head circumference. For anyone outside that assumption, the result is a headset that slips, presses painfully on the temple, or won't sit flat at all, forcing a choice between comfort and being able to use the product.

Insight

That research pointed to three contact points the fit mechanism had to get right:

Temple contact

Highly sensitive: the mechanism has to float or brace without clamping there.

Mastoid anchor

High stability for bone conduction, so the exciter is anchored here with a contour pad.

Back-of-head clamp

Force kept under 1N: enough to hold, not enough to hurt after 60 minutes.

04 · Engineering

A pull-to-slide locking mechanism with a 7mm travel range, detailed down to draft angles and slot tolerances.

Detail design

Materials specified for comfort and durability: LSR rear band (Shore A45-50, sweat-proof), injection-moulded TPU headband, PC-ABS control housing, POM slide rail for smooth, lubrication-free sliding.

05 · Prototype

Foam and cardboard mock-ups, fit-tested on real heads to validate the clamping geometry before committing it to CAD.

Physical testing

06 · Result

The result is a bone-conduction headset that adjusts smoothly across the 5th to 95th percentile of head sizes and sits comfortably over natural afro hair, without the pressure points or slipping that rule out most hearing tech on the market today, validated by hand before it ever reached CAD.
