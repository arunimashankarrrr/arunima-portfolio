The Wave Speaker

A speaker designed around material, supply chain and end of life, not just form

RoleDesign & material procurement
ScopeResearch → material selection → sourcing
ImpactUp to 97% lower embodied carbon
ManufactureUK-only, closed-loop end of life

The Wave Speaker's form takes its cue from a nautilus shell, a shape chosen for how it moves sound, not just how it looks. Every material and supplier behind it was chosen the same way: for what happens to it after use. Recycled aluminium over primary production cut projected carbon footprint by up to 97%. Full disassembly with zero adhesives or soldering keeps every material stream in a closed loop.

01 · Research

"The shell shape looks like a wave of sound: a different semantic to what's already on the market."

Inspiration

02 · Sketches

From the shell's golden-spiral geometry to a driver-and-housing form that actually holds a speaker.

Ideation

03 · Development

Developing the form for manufacture: an early direction wasted too much internal space before converging on the final spiral.

Refinement

04 · Material & CAD

Recycled polypropylene for the housing, recycled aluminium reserved for mounts and supports, sized and detailed for 5-axis CNC and hot extrusion.

Detail design

Designed for full disassembly: no glue or soldering. Metal threaded inserts and 5-axis CNC machining keep every component separable: driver, tweeter, crossover network, amplifier and battery.

05 · Sourcing & Manufacture

Every supplier chosen on the same terms as the materials: UK-based, low-carbon, and built for a closed loop.

Supply chain

Closed-loop by design: Supersede's polypropylene panels are collected via buy-back, reground and re-extruded; recycled aluminium is up to 95% recoverable indefinitely without quality loss. UK-only logistics removes CBAM tariff exposure for EU market entry.

06 · Final

The Wave Speaker: high-fidelity, responsible, aesthetic. A product designed as much by its supply chain as by its shape.
