This page walks through how The Wave Speaker's form and materials were chosen together: a nautilus-shaped shell that shapes how sound moves, built entirely from recycled aluminium and recycled polypropylene, held together without a single drop of glue or solder.

01 · The Problem

Consumer electronics are typically built from a mix of virgin plastics, adhesives and soldered joints that make them almost impossible to take apart, repair or recycle. When a product like a speaker breaks or gets replaced, it usually ends up as mixed landfill waste, and the carbon cost of the virgin aluminium and plastic inside it is already spent. The brief here was the opposite: choose materials and a construction method that stay valuable at every stage, not just the first one.

04 · Material & Engineering

Recycled polypropylene for the housing, recycled aluminium reserved for mounts and supports, sized and detailed for 5-axis CNC and hot extrusion.

Detail design

Designed for full disassembly: no glue or soldering. Metal threaded inserts and 5-axis CNC machining keep every component separable: driver, tweeter, crossover network, amplifier and battery.

05 · Sourcing & End of Life

Every supplier chosen on the same terms as the materials: UK-based, low-carbon, and built for a closed loop.

Supply chain

Closed-loop by design: Supersede's polypropylene panels are collected via buy-back, reground and re-extruded; recycled aluminium is up to 95% recoverable indefinitely without quality loss. UK-only logistics removes CBAM tariff exposure for EU market entry.

06 · Result

The result is a speaker that sounds and looks like a premium product but is built entirely from recycled material, held together without glue or solder, and designed from day one to go back into the loop it came from. High-fidelity. Responsible. Aesthetic.
