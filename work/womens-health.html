Design Research

Women's Health

An ongoing, independent research project into how people track and understand their menstrual health. Still in the discovery phase: this page documents the research, not a finished product.

RoleIndependent research
StageDiscovery & primary research
MethodsCompetitive review, participant interviews
StatusOngoing

01 · Why This, Why Now

Context

Menstrual health is described by health bodies as a vital sign, something that says a lot about a person's overall health, yet conversations about it are still often quiet, and the tools people use to track it vary hugely in how much they actually help someone understand their own body. That gap between how important it is and how openly it gets discussed is the starting point for this project.

02 · Reviewing What Already Exists

Apple's Cycle Tracking, built into iPhone, Apple Watch and the Health app, is one of the most widely used tools for this. The first step was understanding it properly: how it's positioned, how the logging flow actually works day to day, and what it asks someone to pay attention to.

Competitive review

03 · Talking to Real People

Reviewing an existing product only tells part of the story. Alongside it, I reached out directly to people who track their own cycles, posting a short callout on social media and following up with anyone willing to have a conversation about their experience.

Primary research

Posting somewhere people already talk openly turned out to matter: it reached people willing to share honestly about something that's often private, rather than filtering for whoever happened to see a formal recruitment form.

04 · Where This Is Headed

This project is still in its research phase. The next step is turning what's come out of the competitive review and these conversations into a clear set of design opportunities, rather than jumping straight to a solution. I'll update this page as that work develops.
