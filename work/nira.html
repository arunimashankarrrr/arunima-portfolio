Final Year Project · 2026

NiRA

Autonomous countertop cooking appliance

RoleSole designer & engineer
ScopeResearch → CAD → controls
ToolsSolidWorks, Arduino, Raspberry Pi
Subsystems6, detailed through 5+ iterations each

NiRA peels, chops, seasons, stirs and heats, handling the physical labour of home cooking so a warm, real meal is never out of reach after a long day. Designed and detailed end to end across six electro-mechanical subsystems, from first sketch to manufacturing-ready assembly.

01 · Research

Insight

“Food is personal.”

A warm bowl of curry, dal or stew is all you need some days, but it asks for hours in the kitchen. Time poverty, cognitive load and physical labour all stand between people and a home-cooked meal.

Time poverty

Long days leave little time to cook from scratch, let alone plan and prep a meal.

Cognitive load

Deciding what to cook and holding a recipe in your head is its own kind of tired.

Physical labour

Peeling, chopping and standing over a stove asks a lot of a body that's already spent.

02 · Sketches

Working through lid, dispensing and containment mechanisms by hand before anything touched CAD.

Ideation

03 · Development

Two early directions tested before converging on NiRA's single-vessel, top-loading format.

Concepts

04 · CAD & Engineering

Detailed across six subsystems in SolidWorks: iris lid, cooling chamber, spice dispensing, stirring, structure and induction base.

Detail design

Materials specified for food safety and structural load: PEEK 150CA30 for the CNC-machined iris rings, Eastman Tritan TX1001 for food-contact trays, 6061-T6 aluminium for the chassis, and PPS-GF40 for electrical housings.

05 · Interaction

Loading ingredients, setting a timer and starting a cook, backed by a companion app running on Arduino and Raspberry Pi.

In use

06 · Final

NiRA: designed, engineered and detailed from first sketch to a manufacturing-ready, six-subsystem assembly. A real, home-cooked meal, without asking for the hours or the strength it usually takes.
