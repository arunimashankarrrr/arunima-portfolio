Halo Arc

An inclusive bone-conduction headset

RoleDesigner & mechanism engineer
ScopeResearch → fit mechanism → prototype
Fit range5th–95th head percentile
FocusAfro hair & sweat-mapped comfort

Most bone-conduction headsets are designed around a narrow, medicalised idea of "fit." Halo Arc starts from a pull-to-slide adjustment mechanism built to fit across the 5th–95th head-circumference percentile, resolving comfort and contact issues around afro hair and sweat mapping that user research surfaced early on — then iterated through physical prototyping and testing to validate it.

01 — Research

Who this is for, what current hearing solutions get wrong, and where the head actually tolerates contact.

Insight

Temple contact

Highly sensitive — the mechanism has to float or brace without clamping there.

Mastoid anchor

High stability for bone conduction, so the exciter is anchored here with a contour pad.

Back-of-head clamp

Force kept under 1N — enough to hold, not enough to hurt after 60 minutes.

02 — Sketches

Exploring headband geometry, grip points and control placement by hand.

Ideation

03 — Development

"Less medical, more personal" — the earliest concept, developed forward into Halo Arc's final silhouette.

Concepts

04 — CAD & Engineering

A pull-to-slide locking mechanism with a 7mm travel range, detailed down to draft angles and slot tolerances.

Detail design

Materials specified for comfort and durability — LSR rear band (Shore A45-50, sweat-proof), injection-moulded TPU headband, PC-ABS control housing, POM slide rail for smooth, lubrication-free sliding.

05 — Prototype

Foam and cardboard mock-ups, fit-tested on real heads to validate the clamping geometry before committing it to CAD.

Physical testing

06 — Final

Halo Arc — a fit mechanism designed around the people existing hearing tech overlooks, validated by hand before it ever reached CAD.
