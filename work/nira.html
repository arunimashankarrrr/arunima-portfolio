NiRA is an autonomous countertop appliance that peels, chops, seasons, stirs and heats, handling the physical labour of home cooking so a warm, real meal is never out of reach after a long day. This page walks through how it went from a research question about why people stop cooking, to a six-subsystem product detailed end to end in SolidWorks.

01 · The Problem

Insight

“Food is personal.”

A home-cooked meal takes real effort: planning what to make, prepping ingredients, standing over a stove for the best part of an hour. When time, energy or physical ability run short, most people don't stop wanting to eat well, they just stop cooking, reaching for ultra-processed ready meals and takeaways instead. Over time that trades away nutrition, cost and the simple comfort of a proper meal.

Three barriers came up again and again in early research, and NiRA was built to remove each of them:

Time poverty

Long days leave no time to plan, prep and cook from scratch, so cooking is often the first thing to go.

Cognitive load

Deciding what to make and holding a recipe in your head is its own kind of tiring, especially in the evening.

Physical labour

For anyone with reduced strength, grip or mobility, peeling, chopping and stirring can rule out cooking altogether.

04 · Engineering

Detail design

Materials specified for food safety and structural load: PEEK 150CA30 for the CNC-machined iris rings, Eastman Tritan TX1001 for food-contact trays, 6061-T6 aluminium for the chassis, and PPS-GF40 for electrical housings.

06 · Result

The result is a countertop appliance that takes on the peeling, chopping, seasoning, stirring and heating that usually keep a home-cooked meal out of reach, engineered and detailed from first sketch to a manufacturing-ready, six-subsystem assembly.
